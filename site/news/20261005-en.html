PRESS RELEASE · Youth support / AI Mentor

Two in three young people chose a first step after talking with AI

AwakApp publishes findings from a 507-person pilot. 108 of 158 participants from households with fewer resources also chose a concrete action.

Project lead: AwakApp (General Incorporated Association)
Supported by: Water Dragon Foundation / Issued by: AwakApp Japan LLC

View the report slides ↓Read the results

AwakApp, a general incorporated association in Japan, has published findings from AI Mentor, a youth consultation project supported by Water Dragon Foundation. From 1 to 10 August 2026, 507 people aged 15–19 across all 47 prefectures of Japan used AI Mentor. Immediately afterwards, 342 participants, approximately two in three, chose a specific first action to take that day.

In the group classified as having fewer household resources, 108 of 158 participants chose an action. The classification uses nine indicators of daily life, including meals out, learning materials and study conditions. It is not based on income or an official poverty threshold.

AI Mentor has now evolved into SIMY. The findings will inform the development of support for carrying out the steps users choose. AwakApp, the general incorporated association, led the project. AwakApp Japan LLC issues this announcement.

“Chose an action” describes an immediate post-consultation response, not verified subsequent action or persistence.

Turning a larger goal into five minutes of action

AI Mentor is a chat consultation service designed to help young people work through concerns about education or their future and choose something to work on.

The AI presents eight perspectives based on the consultation. Users select two that matter to them, and the service develops a plan in stages. It also suggests a first-week plan and something they can do in five to ten minutes that day.

The published material illustrates a university-admissions consultation in which the first step is to write down mock-exam scores by subject. This example was constructed from actual AI output to explain the service. It is not evidence that a participant carried out the action.

What the 507 participants reported

342 participants (67.5%) chose a concrete action for that day, such as researching, studying or talking to someone.

314 (61.9%) selected at least one of seven responses, including a clearer goal, a new option, information about costs or procedures, clarification of concerns, or deciding what to do that day.

In a separate question, 243 (47.9%) gave responses indicating that they selected among suggestions, adapted a suggestion to their circumstances, or devised another action.

These findings are self-reports collected immediately after consultation. They do not establish whether participants later carried out or sustained the actions.

Outcomes by household resources and remaining questions

Of 463 respondents with classifiable household-resource responses, 158 reported 0–3 items and were classified as having fewer household resources. The other 305 reported 4–9 items. Respectively, 108 (68.4%) and 224 (73.4%) chose an action for that day.

Among those able to describe their consultation concretely afterwards, 59 of 74 (79.7%) and 186 of 230 (80.9%) chose an action. This conditional analysis does not establish that household disparities were eliminated or that AI had equal effects across groups.

Conversely, 84 of 158 (53.2%) and 75 of 305 (24.6%) did not describe their consultation concretely. These post-consultation responses alone do not establish why. The findings identify a need to examine how users proceed through and reflect on consultations.

Applying the AI Mentor findings to SIMY

The project aimed to support young people with limited access to consultation and learning opportunities, including those from single-parent households. Participants included 73 who reported their mother as their main parent or guardian.

The pilot documented the selection of a next step. Further work needs to examine whether users carry it out and connect with people or support programs.

Proposed applications of SIMY to youth support include prompts that help users proceed through a consultation, guidance on scholarships and support services, and assistance with following through. These are future approaches, not functions whose effectiveness was established in this pilot.

METHOD

Method and definitions

Project lead
AwakApp (General Incorporated Association)
Grant support
Water Dragon Foundation
Pilot dates
1–10 August 2026
Participants
507 people aged 15–19 across all 47 prefectures of Japan. High-school students: 71.0%. Female participants: 73.4%. The sample is not representative of all young people.
Implementation support
GMO Research & AI supported the study, which recruited people aged 15–19 across Japan, excluding junior-high-school students, to experience AI consultation and answer questionnaires.
Method
Pre-consultation questionnaire, use of AI Mentor and an immediate post-consultation questionnaire. Participants then read material introducing scholarships and related topics, plus a message from IITH students, and reported their reactions. The main outcomes above use the immediate post-consultation responses.
Household classification
Nine indicators: family meals out, domestic travel, international travel, a family car, cram school, learning materials, extracurricular costs, a study desk, and discussing education costs at home without worry. This is not an income measure or official poverty classification.
Scope
Descriptive analysis of self-reports. No control-group comparison. The results do not establish a causal effect of AI or subsequent action or persistence.
Source
Water Dragon Foundation Project Completion Report: AI Mentor, dated 25 August 2026. Wording and interpretation have been revised for publication while retaining the reported figures.
