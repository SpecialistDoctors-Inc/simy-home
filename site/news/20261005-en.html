PRESS RELEASE · Youth support / AI Mentor

Two in three young people chose a first step after talking with AI

Young people from households with fewer resources also chose a next step

Project lead: AwakApp (General Incorporated Association)
Supported by: Water Dragon Foundation / Issued by: AwakApp Japan LLC

View the report slides ↓Read the results

AwakApp, a general incorporated association, conducted the AI Mentor project with support from Water Dragon Foundation. From 1 to 10 August 2026, 507 young people aged 15–19 across all 47 prefectures of Japan used the service. Immediately after consultation, 342 chose a specific first action to take that day.

The project offered young people a chance to work through their concerns and consider a next step, across family backgrounds. AI Mentor has now evolved into SIMY.

“Chose a first step” means selecting a concrete action immediately after consultation. It does not measure whether participants later carried out or sustained that action.

108 of 158 young people from households with fewer resources chose a first step

Among 158 participants classified as having fewer household resources using nine daily-life indicators, 108 (68.4%) chose a first step. The corresponding result for other households was 224 of 305 (73.4%).

Among participants able to describe their consultation in concrete terms afterwards, 59 of 74 (79.7%) in the lower-resource group chose a first step, compared with 186 of 230 (80.9%) in the other group. Helping young people who struggle to put their concerns into words remains a priority.

AI Mentor has evolved into SIMY

This pilot assessed whether participants chose a first step for today. SIMY aims to help them turn that choice into a plan: when to act, where to begin and whom to involve. The findings will inform support that connects AI conversations with people and support programs.

METHOD

Methodology and definitions

Pilot dates
1–10 August 2026
Participants
507 young people aged 15–19 across all 47 prefectures of Japan
Method
Pre-consultation questionnaire, use of AI Mentor and an immediate post-consultation questionnaire
Household resource classification
Participants reporting 0–3 of nine daily-life resources were classified as having fewer household resources. The items cover meals out, travel, a car, tutoring, learning materials, extracurricular activities, study conditions and education costs. Of 463 valid responses, 158 fell into this group. This is not a classification based on household income or an official poverty threshold.
Scope of the results
The results are based on participant self-reports. High-school students accounted for 71.0% and female participants for 73.4%. The sample is not representative of all young people. There was no control-group comparison.
